Re: Early-Career Researchers' Response to STFC Prioritisation Outcomes for the Multidisciplinary Facilities User Communities

Dear Professor Dougherty and Professor Sir Ian Chapman,

The scientific community has already expressed deep concern over the proposed cuts to the operations of the Multidisciplinary Facilities (MDFs) such as ISIS, Diamond, and the Central Laser Facility; however, the specific impact on early-career researchers (ECRs) that rely on these facilities has not been discussed. 
For many of us, access to these facilities has been central to completing our PhDs, publishing our first papers, building collaborations, and securing our next positions. 
In this letter, signed by and on behalf of members of the ECR community, we share our concerns that these cuts will harm the career development of talented ECRs, force UK researchers to look abroad for MDF access and opportunities, and weaken the pipeline of UK talent in crucial areas including both curiosity- and application-driven research.

MDFs directly support the growth and development of ECRs in ways that cannot be replicated in other settings. 
This is evident not only from the PhD studentships they fund, but also in the training and mentoring opportunities they provide. 
These include hands-on training at user experiments, networking opportunities created at MDF-organised user meetings, and MDF-supported workshops and training schools.
For many, these activities mark the beginning of long-term working relationships between ECRs and the facility scientists who develop, operate and continually advance these world-leading experimental techniques. 
They also foster collaborations among ECRs, creating new, strong, and diverse research networks across disciplines. 
Critically, all of these opportunities equip ECRs to become the highly technical workforce that is required for UK advanced industries. 
The current proposed cuts to facility operations will directly obstruct knowledge and skills transfer for ECRs, with particular impact on new user communities and those at the start of their research careers, leading to a loss of talent at the very beginning of the career pipeline.

Unlike established investigators, ECRs typically work on fixed, short-term contracts with little job security, and thus rely on the timely access to MDFs for thesis and publication completion to enable future employment. 
Reduced operation will inevitably lead to fewer experimental opportunities and increased competition for access to the MDFs, slowing the pace of scientific discovery and delaying publications of high impact research beyond the end of an ECR’s contract, thereby reducing the chance of securing subsequent positions. 
In addition, when compared to established principal investigators, ECRs may also be less competitive for oversubscribed experimental opportunities and funding. 
This again reduces ECR opportunities, not only to collect the high-quality data required to deliver world-class science, but also to develop the technical skills and scientific experience required to progress towards independent careers. 

The UK has already invested substantially in training ECRs. 
Reducing their ability to access the facilities on which their research depends risks losing out on the return on that investment just as they begin to establish independent careers. 
With reduced access to the MDFs, accessing competitive grant funding, both within the UK and abroad, will be more challenging, leaving UK researchers disadvantaged compared to our international peers. 
Further, with uncertainty over the existence of certain techniques and programmes, such as the muon instruments at ISIS and the Lasers for Science at the Central Laser Facility, it seems likely that many promising projects will be deprioritised by other funding agencies, leaving talented ECRs unable to continue their research careers and unable to transition to other areas of research. 
Once again, the inevitable impact of these cuts will be a loss of UK scientists in both academic and industrial settings in strategically important areas.

We urge you to reconsider the scale of the cuts to the MDFs and embed consultation with ECRs in any future cost-saving procedures. 
Where reprioritisation must occur, we ask for the explicit implementation of safeguards to protect ECRs from becoming the unintended casualty of reduced MDF operations. 
Protecting the UK's reputation as a science superpower requires not only world-class infrastructure, but the retention of the people equipped to run it.

Yours sincerely,
